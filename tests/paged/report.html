Notes on Paged Media

A short report on fragmentation for print

Prepared for the layout working group · November 2026

Contents

  2. Breaks

1. Pages

Paged media has been part of CSS since its second level, yet most of what a book designer asks of a page has only recently become something a browser engine can do. This report looks at what a print engine needs beyond a screen: page boxes, breaks, and the small rules that decide where a line ends up.

A page is a fragmentainer: a box of a given block size that content flows into until it is full. What does not fit goes on in the next one, and the engine has to choose where. It prefers breaks between blocks over breaks between lines, and both over a break inside an element that asked to be kept whole.

Orphans and widows guard a paragraph's ends: a single line stranded at the bottom of a page, or alone at the top of the next, reads badly. The defaults ask for two lines on each side, and an engine that cannot honour them looks for an earlier break instead.

2. Breaks

A page is a fragmentainer: a box of a given block size that content flows into until it is full. What does not fit goes on in the next one, and the engine has to choose where. It prefers breaks between blocks over breaks between lines, and both over a break inside an element that asked to be kept whole.

Orphans and widows guard a paragraph's ends: a single line stranded at the bottom of a page, or alone at the top of the next, reads badly. The defaults ask for two lines on each side, and an engine that cannot honour them looks for an earlier break instead.

Margins at a break are truncated. A heading's space above it makes sense in the middle of a page, but at the top of a new one it would only push the text down; after a forced break, though, the space is kept, since the author asked for the page to start there.

Borders and backgrounds of a box split across pages are sliced by default: the box looks as if it had been cut with scissors, its top border on the first page and its bottom border on the last. The alternative, cloning, draws the whole decoration on every fragment.

A good page break is one the reader never notices.
  • breaks between blocks first
  • then between lines
  • inside an avoid only as a last resort
